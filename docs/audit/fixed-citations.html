A2. Mikheev survey percentages did not exist in the paper

Severity: High · Location: journal.typ:70–73, Introduction · Fixed: 18 November 2026

Problem. The Introduction said: “In our previous survey of 213 M/EEG researchers, 52% of respondents reported having previously created topoplots. This is the second results after conventional ERP waveforms (61%) @mikheev2024art.” Neither number appears in the paper as a plotting rate. 52% occurs nowhere; the only 61% is an unrelated result (61% of 185 respondents read a topoplot series as time-point snapshots). The ranking was right.

Evidence. Figure 6 of Mikheev et al. (2024), PDF p. 9 (caption anchor, archived PDF): 175 of 213 respondents had plotted a topoplot (82%) and 187 of 213 an ERP plot (88%). These counts exist only as bar labels in the chart, so they are not in the extracted text, which is why text-based checks could not catch the error.

Fix.

  • The sentence now reads: “In our previous survey of 213 M/EEG researchers, 82% of respondents (175) reported having previously plotted a topoplot, second only to conventional ERP waveform plots (88%, 187) @mikheev2024art.” It carries a source comment, // Source: mikheev2024art, Fig. 6 (PDF p. 9) …, which does not print. journal.pdf was recompiled with scripts/compile-journal.ps1.
  • Prevention: the Figure 6 counts for all eight plot types were added to the wiki as evidence claim mikheev-plot-familiarity, noting that they must be checked against the figure. The wiki was rebuilt and validated.

A17. Petek: “quantifying” counted rows where tools only draw

Severity: High · Location: journal.typ, Table 1, Petek et al. (2026) row · Fixed: 18 November 2026

Problem. The cell said: “For only 5 of the 18 visualizations did at least one tool provide built-in support for quantifying uncertainty. For the remaining 13 (72%), users had to construct the visual encoding themselves.” Petek et al. rate each tool on each visualization as ✓ (the tool quantifies uncertainty and draws it), ◦ (the user quantifies, the tool draws) or × (the user does both). Only 3 rows have a ✓; on 2 more the best any tool offers is ◦, so users still quantify there. “5 … quantifying” was therefore wrong, and the second sentence left out that the 13 remaining rows also need the user to quantify. The 13/18 (72%) count was correct. This error appeared after the 26 October Table 1 audit.

Evidence. Petek et al. (2026), Table 3 and its legend, PDF p. 18 (raw passage, archived PDF): ✓ rows are confidence interval for mean, point-wise confidence band around a regression line, and bar chart with confidence interval for mean; ◦-only rows are uncertain binary heat map and uncertain bivariate means; the other 13 rows are × for all five tools.

Fix. The cell now reads: “Only 3 of the 18 visualizations had a tool that both quantified and drew the uncertainty; for 2 more, tools drew it but users had to quantify it. For the remaining 13 (72%), users had to do both themselves.” A source comment with the row counts sits under the cell and does not print. journal.pdf was recompiled with scripts/compile-journal.ps1; the rendered table was checked.

A41. ERP error bands were called “common”

Severity: Moderate · Location: journal.typ:293–295, Related Work (Uncertainty in EEG/ERP Visualization) · Fixed: 24 November 2026

Problem. The sentence said: “In ERP waveforms, uncertainty is commonly shown using error bars or shaded confidence intervals @allen2012data @kappenman2021erp @pernet2011limo.” The three sources only give examples, and none says how common the practice is. Kappenman shades ±1 SEM, not a confidence interval; Pernet shows bootstrap 95% confidence intervals; Allen describes the typical ERP panel as having “no portrayal of uncertainty” and adds 95% bands as a recommendation. Mikheev et al. (2024), where 40% of ERP plots had no error bars, points the other way.

Fix. The sentence now reads: “In ERP waveforms, uncertainty can be shown, for example, with error bars or shaded bands for the standard error or a confidence interval @allen2012data @kappenman2021erp @pernet2011limo, although many published ERP plots omit it @mikheev2024art.” It no longer claims a prevalence, covers both the SEM bands and the confidence intervals the sources show, and adds the counterpoint from Mikheev et al. (2024). journal.pdf was recompiled with scripts/compile-journal.ps1.

A36. Perceptual studies were cited to a probability-judgment study

Severity: Moderate · Location: journal.typ:269–276, Related Work (Evaluating Uncertainty Visualizations) · Fixed: 25 November 2026

Problem. Perceptual studies that “identify areas with high uncertainty or compare its magnitude across different areas, typically measuring accuracy and response time” were cited to Hullman et al. (2015). That study asked people to estimate properties of one to three distributions, with no spatial search. The description fits Sanyal et al. (2009). After the citation was corrected (24 Nov), the next sentence, “Both approaches provide objective measures”, had no antecedent: the overview names four kinds of evaluation, but the probability-judgment studies were no longer described.

Fix. The perceptual sentence cites sanyal2009user (24 Nov). A sentence on probability-judgment studies, cited to Hullman et al. (2015), now comes before “Both approaches” (25 Nov): “Whereas perceptual studies ask where uncertainty is high, probability-judgment studies ask participants to estimate properties of the depicted distributions, such as how likely one value is to exceed another, and score these estimates against the true values @hullman2015hypothetical.” “Both approaches” now refers to the two approaches just described. journal.pdf was recompiled with scripts/compile-journal.ps1.

C43. Correll 2018 E2: VSUPs ranked best despite higher danger

Severity: High · Location: supplement.typ:381, 385, S3, Correll 2018 · Source: Correll, Moritz & Heer 2018 · Verdict: Not supported · Fixed: 18 November 2026

Was. Best “E2: VSUP + wedge legend ≈ VSUP + square legend”; others “Traditional bivariate + wedge ≈ square” (i.e., VSUP > traditional)

Evidence. raw/correll2018task.md#p007-b008: no effect of quantization on mean placement uncertainty (p = .83); the KS test shows VSUP users avoided very-high-uncertainty cells. #p008-b002: VSUP placements had higher average danger, i.e. lower expected value (p = .02). Fix. Best → *E2:* no clear winner: VSUPs reduced very-high-uncertainty placements but produced higher average danger than traditional bivariate maps; legend shape had no effect.; others → *E2:* not ranked (trade-off between avoiding high uncertainty and expected value).
