F12. Task 1 text overstated weak effects (was U5)

Severity: High · Area: Task 1 reporting

Problem. The text said Bivariate corner and Bivariate range “were associated with lower odds” of a correct answer, although every interval included 1 and the omnibus test gave p = .056.

Resolution (18 Nov 2026). All accuracy prose on the Task 1 page comes from one helper (task1_effect_sentence). A difference is called lower or higher only when its interval excludes the null; otherwise the text says the interval includes it and names the weakest estimates. This covers the coefficient and conditional-logit conclusions and a new section, “Summary of the Task 1 accuracy evidence”.

Current numbers.

  • Omnibus test: χ²(6) = 12.30, p = .056.
  • Population-average contrasts with Basic (Dunnett-adjusted):
    • Bivariate range: −4.4 pp [−10.6, +1.9], p = .27.
    • Bivariate corner: −4.2 pp [−10.5, +2.1], p = .31.
  • Odds ratios (Dunnett-adjusted): Bivariate range 0.71 [0.44, 1.15]; Bivariate corner 0.72 [0.45, 1.16].
  • The page calls the evidence weak: Range and Corner may carry a small accuracy cost that the study cannot tell apart from no difference.

F13. Task 2 “detection score” was μ, not the expected score (was U6)

Severity: Moderate · Area: Task 2 reporting

Problem. emmeans(type = "response") on the ordered-beta model returns μ, the mean of the continuous part. It ignores the probability mass at 0 and 1.

Resolution (18 Nov 2026). The page now reports E[y] = P(1) + (1 − P(0) − P(1)) μ. It is computed from the glmmTMB cutpoints (psi), which were checked against the observed shares of exact 0s and 1s. Intervals come from 4,000 draws of the model parameters. The estimates are averaged over the participant, stimulus and image effects (primary), and also given with those effects set to zero. Tables, the figure and the text use E[y].

Basic topoplot Value
μ (old label “estimated detection score”) 68.3%
E[y], random effects at zero 73.3%
E[y], averaged over random effects 71.2% [66.0, 75.7]
Observed mean 69.8%

(The audit’s μ of 67.6% came from a fit without the image intercept.)

F15. Rating models: proportional odds and random slopes (was U9)

Severity: Moderate · Area: Subjective-rating models

Problem. The rating models (rating_ord ~ vistype + (1 | ID)) let participants differ only in their overall rating level, although every participant rated every visualization and could also differ in which visualizations they preferred (random slopes). The models also assume proportional odds (a visualization shifts all rating thresholds by the same amount); likelihood-ratio tests rejected this for all four questions (p = .011 to 3 × 10⁻⁵).

Resolution (18 Nov 2026). A new section on the Subjective page, “Model checks and sensitivity analyses”, covers three points.

  • Proportional odds. A likelihood-ratio test (fixed-effects clm with and without visualization-specific thresholds) rejects proportional odds for all 4 items (p < .001 to .011). The page therefore reports model-smoothed mean ratings (largest difference from the raw means: 0.08) and does not interpret odds ratios.
  • Random slopes. Each participant rated each visualization once per item, so (1 + vistype | ID) would have one observation per random effect. It cannot be separated from the latent observation-level error, so the random intercept is the maximal identifiable structure. The page explains this.
  • Sensitivity. A linear mixed model rating ~ vistype + (1 | ID) with the same Dunnett contrasts agrees with the ordinal model on whether each interval excludes zero for 24 of 24 contrasts.

F16. Composite ranking and “leader” highlighting (was U10)

Severity: Moderate · Area: Subjective ratings

Problem. The overall ranking summed the mean ratings of the four questions with equal weight, although they measure different things (one is likelihood of use, F10; one was asked at a different time, F11), and neighbouring ranks were indistinguishable (e.g., Uncertainty markers 12.28 [11.62, 12.88] vs Basic 12.24 [11.70, 12.82]). The tables also highlighted a “leader” per question, e.g. Confidence intervals for comparison, although its difference from Basic (+0.18 [−0.09, 0.45]) included zero and its differences from the runners-up were never tested.

Resolution (18 Nov 2026).

  • The “Sum of mean ratings” column is removed. The page explains why: the items measure different things (F10) and were asked at different times (F11).
  • is_leader is replaced by excludes_zero. Red now marks contrasts whose Dunnett-adjusted interval excludes zero, for example Confidence intervals for comparison (+0.18 [−0.09, 0.45]) is no longer highlighted.
  • A generated list under the figure names the contrasts that exclude zero. The page states that differences among the uncertainty visualizations were not tested, so it names no “best” visualization.

F17. Time outliers and the time estimand (was U11)

Severity: Moderate · Area: Task 1 and Task 2 completion time

Problem. The descriptive time tables and the models used different data: the tables dropped outlier trials (1.5 × IQR per visualization), while the models kept every trial, including pages left open for 5,264 s and 11,221 s. The model estimate was also labelled “estimated time”, although after back-transforming from the log scale it is a geometric mean, i.e. the median time of a typical participant.

Resolution (18 Nov 2026).

  • One rule for tables and models. Trial pages longer than 180 s are excluded, and the same trials feed the tables, the violin plot, the position checks, the time models and the time–accuracy models. The median page time is 12–13 s, so a page open for more than 3 minutes almost certainly means an interruption. The page says the cap was chosen after seeing the distributions.

    Trials removed Participants affected
    Task 1 19 of 3,066 (0.6%) 11
    Task 2 21 of 3,066 (0.7%) 15

    The old per-visualization 1.5 × IQR rule removed 8.3% and 7.3% of trials, and only from the tables.

  • Estimand. Model estimates are labelled “Geometric mean time, typical participant (s)” everywhere, with a paragraph explaining the estimand.

  • Sensitivity (collapsed): refits with all trials and with the IQR rule. Only one result changes: Task 1 Uncertainty markers is slower than Basic under the cap and with all trials, but not under the IQR rule (1.09 [0.99, 1.20]). The time–accuracy findings hold under all three rules.

F21. glmmTMB was built against an older TMB (was U17)

Severity: Low · Area: R environment, all glmmTMB models

Problem. Loading the packages warned that glmmTMB 1.1.14 was built with TMB 1.9.21 while TMB 1.9.22 was installed.

Resolution (18 Nov 2026). Rtools is not installed, so a source build was not possible. Instead the current CRAN Windows binaries were installed as a matched pair: TMB 1.9.25 and glmmTMB 1.1.15.2 (built against it). The mismatch warning is gone. All page caches were cleared and the whole site was re-rendered from scratch.

Check. The visible text of every page (prose and tables) was compared before and after. Task 1, Task 2, Hit or miss, Time, Sample and Feedback are identical. The only change was two interval bounds in the Subjective page’s linear-mixed-model sensitivity table (±0.01). That came from the Monte Carlo integration behind adjust = "mvt", not from glmmTMB. Seeds are now set before every mvt contrast on that page, so it reproduces exactly.

F19. Sample definition and data quality (was U14)

Severity: Low · Area: Data preparation

Problem. The sample definition was not documented: four completed records from 23 Jul 2026 (probably pilot runs) were excluded by a date cutoff without explanation. Response quality had never been checked: five participants exceeded SoSci’s speeding index (TIME_RSI > 2), and two gave the same rating to all 28 rating items.

Resolution (18 Nov 2026). A new script, scripts/data_quality.R, documents the sample definition and a quality screen. The Sample page has a new section, “Sample definition and data quality”, with all numbers generated inline.

  • Launch date. Records started before 24 Jul 2026 are excluded: 4 completed test runs, all from 23 Jul 2026. A further 179 records started after launch were not completed.
  • Quality screen (flagged, not excluded): 5 participants with TIME_RSI > 2 and 2 who gave the same rating to all 28 rating items; 7 in total.
  • Sensitivity (Subjective page): without these 7, the rating contrasts against Basic move by at most 0.07, and 0 of 24 intervals change their position relative to zero.

F20. Stale hard-coded text and suspicious items (was U15)

Severity: Low · Area: Task 1 and Task 2 pages

Problem. Four unrelated small issues:

  • Task 2 score-distribution caption: said 2,394 scores, a number typed in at an earlier sample size; at N = 146 there were 3,066.
  • Task 2 random-slope note: gave a position-slope SD of 0.006 that was typed in by hand and computed by no code (a refit gave 0.013), and mentioned an alternative model that did not exist.
  • Task 2 estimate figure: the x-axis started at a hard-coded 0.58, just below the lowest interval (0.589), so new data could silently cut intervals off.
  • Task 1 items below chance (50%): target MMN_19 had 41.6% accuracy and one pair 9% (n = 11). Below-chance accuracy can mean the answer key is wrong.

Resolution (18 Nov 2026).

  • Task 2 score-distribution caption. The count is now generated (3,066).
  • Task 2 random-slope callout. The position-slope SD is now computed from a fitted (1 + position_c | ID) model: 0.013 per trial, against a fixed effect of 0.002. The unsupported claim that a diagonal visualization model “remains available” is removed.
  • Task 2 axes. The estimate and gradient figures take their x-ranges from the data.
  • Task 1 below-chance items. These are not mis-keyed: all 3,066 trials pass an answer-key consistency check. They are hard MMN targets that resemble some N170 maps. The diagnostic is a collapsed section on the Task 1 page; the follow-up is U16.

F9. Completion times were joined to the wrong trials

Severity: Critical · Area: Task 1 and Task 2 completion time

Problem. SoSci Survey records “Time spent on page xxx”, and each trial page keeps its questionnaire number when the display order is randomized. The pages treated TIME005:TIME025 and TIME029:TIME049 as times at presentation positions, so every time was attached to the wrong trial. All time tables, violin plots, time models, and the Task 1 time–accuracy model were wrong.

Resolution.

  • Times are now joined on trial = page − 4 (Task 1) and trial = page − 28 (Task 2). The presentation position comes from the logged display order.
  • Both analyses moved to the new Time analysis page. The Task 1 and Task 2 trial reconstructions are shared scripts (scripts/task1_trials.R, scripts/task2_trials.R).
  • Each task has a visible check plot of time by presentation position. It shows the expected learning curve: the first trial shown is the slowest (Task 2: about 31 s, falling to about 11 s).
  • The Task 1 time–accuracy sentences are now generated from the confidence intervals.

Corrected results (geometric-mean time for a typical participant; updated 18 Nov 2026 for the 180 s outlier cap, see F17):

Fastest Slowest
Task 1 Basic 10.5 s Bivariate corner 14.6 s, Adjacent 14.2 s
Task 2 Basic 10.2 s Bivariate corner 16.3 s

Task 1 time–accuracy, corrected:

F2. Task 1 ignored repeated comparison items

Severity: High · Area: Task 1 accuracy model

Problem. The accuracy model treated responses as independent given the participant, although target and distractor images recur across participants.

Resolution.

  • The primary model is correct ~ vistype + pattern + position_c + (1 | ID) + (1 | stimulus) + (1 | wrong_image).
  • Reported probabilities are averaged over the three ERP patterns and integrated over the participant, target, and distractor distributions.
  • The Gauss–Hermite integration matches numerical integration to within 2 × 10⁻⁷.
  • A participant-specific position slope was singular and is documented.
