U16. Two MMN targets are hard to tell from N170

Severity: Low · Area: Task 1 stimuli, manuscript

Problem. The below-chance items from F20 are not mis-keyed: every trial passes the answer-key check. MMN_19 (41.6% correct as target) and MMN_29 (45.5%) fail only against N170 distractors (15.5% and 20.5% correct), not against P3 distractors. Both are weak maps (±1.5 and ±1.7 µV) with lateral rather than posterior positivity, and some N170 variations (N170_7, N170_12, N170_16) look like the MMN template. Across all MMN targets, accuracy is 56.3% against N170 distractors and 85.8% against P3 distractors.

The hard items are spread evenly across visualizations, so they add noise rather than bias. They stay in the primary analysis.

Exploratory refit (items chosen after seeing the data): dropping the two targets moves the omnibus test from p = .056 to p = .028. The Bivariate corner and range odds ratios become 0.64 and 0.65, with Dunnett-adjusted intervals still including 1 (adjusted p = .11 and .12).

To decide.

  • Whether the manuscript mentions the exploratory refit.
  • The Stimuli section should say that some single-subject MMN and N170 maps look alike.

U18. Hit-rate probabilities are for a “typical” image, not expected rates

Severity: Moderate · Area: pages/hit_miss.qmd:199–251; pages/task2.qmd:1626–1657 (two-part table, “Probability of exact peak”)

Problem. emmeans(type = "response") on the binomial GLMMs sets all three random intercepts to zero. Their SDs are large (participant 0.97, stimulus 1.35, image 0.81). The table therefore shows the probability for a median participant and image, right under the observed shares, and far below the expected rate. It also inflates relative effects: Adjacent vs Basic is 13.0% vs 5.7% conditional but 22.3% vs 13.3% marginal. This is the problem fixed for the Task 2 score in F13, still present in the hit models.

Basic topoplot Table (RE = 0) Marginal Observed
Exact hit 5% [3, 11] 13.3% 15.1%
Hit or near hit 26% 35.3% 38.4%

Fix. Report marginal probabilities by integrating over the summed random-intercept variance, as Task 2 does for E[y]. Show the conditional values second, or drop them. See Gelman, Hill & Vehtari (2020), §14.4, on predictions evaluated at a central value.

U19. Hit or miss page skips the Dunnett rule

Severity: Moderate · Area: pages/hit_miss.qmd:205–215 (adjust = "none"), :229 and :265 (p < .05 shown in red); pages/time.qmd:387–390; A1, A2

Refitted with adjust = "mvt":

Hit or near hit, Uncertainty markers .029 .124

The exact-hit model is the same as task2_peak_hit_model, but the two pages frame it differently:

  • Add the omnibus p to the Task 2 two-part section.
  • Update the unadjusted .006 and .046 quoted in A1 and A2. Only the Adjacent exact-hit contrast survives adjustment (p = .028).

U21. Rating models: test ignores clustering; estimates are conditional

Severity: Low · Area: pages/subjective.qmd:258–270, :321–327

Problem.

  • The proportional-odds test uses fixed-effects clm, which treats each participant’s 7 ratings per item as independent. Its p values (< .001 to .011) are therefore too small, so “rejects proportional odds for 4 of 4” is overstated. The conclusion (do not interpret odds ratios) is conservative and still holds.
  • mean.class from clmm is evaluated at a participant effect of 0. It is not a population average; it is close to the raw means (≤ 0.08) here only empirically.

Fix.

  • Say that the test ignores clustering, so its p values are approximate.
  • Label the estimates “expected rating, typical participant”.

See Gelman, Hill & Vehtari (2020), §15.5.
