U3. A303 measures likelihood of use, not helpfulness

Severity: High · Area: Subjective ratings, manuscript

Problem. The survey label of A303 is “Usage material”, and its scale runs from “very unlikely” to “very likely”. pages/subjective.qmd and journal.typ call it “helpful for representation” and describe it as helpfulness for representing signal and uncertainty.

Fix. Take the exact item text from the questionnaire and relabel it in the pages, figures, and manuscript.

U4. A301 was asked after Task 1 only

Severity: High · Area: Subjective ratings, manuscript

Problem. A301 (“helpful for comparison”) is on the page after the Task 1 trials. A302–A304 come after Task 2.

Evidence. 23 respondents who stopped before the end of Task 2 had answered A301_01; none had answered A302.

Consequence.

  • The manuscript Procedure, which places all ratings after both tasks, is wrong.
  • The four criteria were measured at different points in the study (see U10).

Fix. Correct the Procedure section and describe the timing of A301 in the methods.

U5. Task 1 text overstates weak or contradicted effects

Severity: High · Area: Task 1 reporting

Problem. The text says Bivariate corner and Bivariate range “were associated with lower odds” of a correct answer. The evidence:

  • odds ratios 0.72 [0.50, 1.04], p = .084, and 0.71 [0.49, 1.03], p = .073;
  • omnibus LRT p = .056;
  • every population-average contrast interval includes zero;
  • conditional-logit sensitivity p = .068 and .055.

Already done. The time–accuracy sentences on the Time analysis page are now generated from the confidence intervals.

Fix. Generate the accuracy sentences on the Task 1 page from the estimates as well.

U6. “Estimated detection score” is not the expected score

Severity: Moderate · Area: Task 2 reporting

Problem. For the ordered-beta family, emmeans(type = "response") returns μ, the mean of the continuous beta part. It ignores the probability mass at 0 and 1. The expected score is E[y] = P(1) + (1 − P(0) − P(1)) μ.

Basic topoplot Value
Reported (μ) 67.6%
E[y], random effects at zero 72.8%
E[y], averaged over random effects 71.0%
Observed mean 69.8%

Contrasts are almost unchanged; the levels and the label “on the detection-score scale” are wrong.

Fix. Compute E[y] from the cutpoints, with delta-method or simulation intervals. State whether it is conditional on the random effects or averaged over them.

U7. Task 2 scores are not comparable across stimuli

Severity: Moderate · Area: Task 2 outcome

Problem. Region values are min–max rescaled within each stimulus, so the same score means different things for different stimuli. For example, the 10th-best region scores between 0.24 and 0.90 depending on the stimulus.

Evidence.

  • Rank outcome: Bivariate corner −0.21, p = .002.
  • Top-3 logistic outcome: no visualization effect reaches p < .05.

Fix. Report a rank or top-k sensitivity analysis, and avoid interpreting score magnitudes across stimuli.

Open question. For 4 of 10 P3 stimuli, the most chosen region (R25) ranks only 10th–14th by score. Participants may target the voltage peak rather than the SNR peak. That is a construct-validity question for the scoring.

U8. Multiplicity is handled inconsistently

Severity: Moderate · Area: All inferential results

Problem. No primary contrasts or multiplicity families are defined, and the same contrast is shown under different adjustments.

  • Task 2, Bivariate corner vs Basic:
    • Tukey table: p = .070.
    • Dunnett figure: interval [−7.2, −0.3] pp, which excludes zero.
    • Coefficient table: unadjusted p = .005.
  • Task 1: unadjusted coefficient p-values are reported.
  • Ratings: the contrast figure is Dunnett-adjusted but labelled only “95% confidence intervals”. Four outcomes × six contrasts have no stated family.

Fix. Choose the primary contrasts and one adjustment family (for example, Dunnett against Basic within each outcome). Use it in every table and figure and name it in captions. Report all estimates, including null results.

U9. Rating models: intercept only, proportional odds violated

Severity: Moderate · Area: Subjective-rating models

Problem.

  • The cumulative-link models use rating_ord ~ vistype + (1 | ID), although each participant rates every visualization.
  • Likelihood-ratio tests on fixed-effects clm fits reject proportional odds for all four questions (p = .011 to 3 × 10⁻⁵).
  • Model-based expected ratings differ from raw means by at most 0.08.

Fix.

  • Evaluate participant-specific visualization effects, or justify the simpler structure with a sensitivity analysis.
  • Report the proportional-odds check, and describe the estimates as model-smoothed means.

U10. Composite ranking and “leader” highlighting

Severity: Moderate · Area: Subjective ratings

Problem.

  • Composite. “Sum of mean ratings” adds four separately measured criteria with equal weight. One of them is a likelihood-of-use item (U3), and one was asked at a different time (U4).
    • With participant-bootstrap intervals, neighbouring ranks overlap. For example, Uncertainty markers 12.28 [11.62, 12.88] vs Basic 12.24 [11.70, 12.82].
  • Leader highlighting. is_leader highlights Confidence intervals for “comparison”. Its difference from Basic is +0.18 [−0.09, 0.45], and its differences from the runners-up were never tested.

Fix. Remove the composite, or show it with intervals and label it as an exploratory equal-weight index. Highlight a leader only if pairwise contrasts support it.

U11. Time outliers and the time estimand

Severity: Moderate · Area: Task 1 and Task 2 completion time

Problem.

  • Different data. The descriptive tables on the Time analysis page drop trials with a per-visualization 1.5 × IQR rule. The models keep everything, including pages of 5,264 s and 11,221 s.
  • Mislabelled estimate. The back-transformed model estimate is a geometric mean (median time for a typical participant), but it is labelled “estimated time”.

Fix.

  • Define one outlier rule in advance and apply it to tables and models.
  • Show the alternative as a sensitivity analysis.
  • Label the estimate as a geometric mean.

U12. Qualitative coding is not validated

Severity: Moderate · Area: Open-ended feedback

Problem. The A305 coding comes from a single LLM coder, with no second coder or agreement statistic.

  • Rule not applied. Generic “bivariate plots” comments are sometimes coded to both Corner and Range instead of General bivariates (IDs 7, 41, 77).
  • Duplicated units. 51 of 298 rows (17%) are one unit duplicated across visualizations.
  • Scale use. Only 2 of 298 units are coded 0.
  • Sparse cells. 31 of 69 theme × visualization rows rest on two or fewer participants, yet are shown with means to two decimals.

Fix.

  • Have a human double-code at least a subsample and report agreement.
  • Enforce the General-bivariates rule.
  • Flag or suppress cells with n ≤ 2.

U13. Fixed item order in the rating questions

Severity: Moderate (design limitation) · Area: Subjective ratings

Problem. Within every rating question the visualizations appear in the same order: Adjacent first, Basic always last. Order and context effects cannot be separated from visualization effects.

Fix. Report this as a limitation in the manuscript.

U14. Sample definition and data quality are undocumented

Severity: Low · Area: Data preparation

Problem.

  • Excluded by the date cutoff: four completed records dated 2026-07-23, probably pilot runs, are excluded without documentation.
  • Possible quality problems:
    • five participants exceed SoSci’s speeding index (TIME_RSI > 2);
    • two gave the same rating to all 28 rating items.
  • Excluding these seven moves rating contrasts by at most 0.07 and changes no interval’s position relative to zero.

Fix. Document the date cutoff and a quality screen, and report the sensitivity analysis.

U15. Stale hard-coded text and suspicious items

Severity: Low · Area: Task 1 and Task 2 pages

Problem.

  • Task 2 score-distribution caption: says 2,394 scores; there are 3,066.
  • Task 2 random-slope callout: states a position-slope SD of 0.006 that no code on the page computes; a refit gives 0.013.
  • Task 2 estimate figure: x-limit hard-coded at 0.58, while the lowest interval bound is 0.589.
  • Task 1 items below chance: MMN_19 as target has 41.6% accuracy, and one pair is at 9% (n = 11).

Fix.

  • Compute the numbers and limits in code.
  • Check the below-chance items for mislabeled or ambiguous stimuli.
