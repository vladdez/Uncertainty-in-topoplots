### Summary

Most important issues:

1. **A4 (High), `journal.typ:86–89`.** The Introduction says Allen's 20% applies to "data with more than three features". Allen's "3D" graphics have exactly three variables (two independent and one dependent; Fig. 1A). The 20% is correct, but the population it describes is misstated.
2. **A36 (Moderate), `journal.typ:266–268`.** Perceptual studies that "identify areas with high uncertainty … accuracy and response time" are attributed to Hullman et al. (2015). That HOPs study asked people to estimate properties of 1–3 distributions, with no spatial search. The description fits Sanyal et al. (2009), which is already in `refs.bib`. *Update 24 Nov 2026:* re-cited to `sanyal2009user` (now `journal.typ:269–271`); the "Both approaches" sentence that follows still needs rephrasing.
3. *Fixed 24 Nov 2026, see [Fixed citations](fixed-citations.md#a41).* **A41 (Moderate), `journal.typ:291–292`.** "In ERP waveforms, uncertainty is *commonly* shown using error bars or shaded confidence intervals". The three cited sources give examples (Kappenman: ±1 SEM shading, not a CI; Pernet: bootstrap 95% CI; Allen: a *recommended* CI band, with the typical ERP panel described as having "no portrayal of uncertainty"). None of them shows how common this practice is. Mikheev's 40% without error bars points the other way.
4. **A8 (Low), Table 1, Allen row.** The 26 Oct audit's single recommended wording correction ("Only 20% of audited 3D graphics portrayed uncertainty") has still not been applied.

Most important issues:

1. **Sanyal et al., Table S3 (High).** Two lower-order rankings contradict or go beyond
   the paper's Table 1 (PDF p. 7). The paper reports only significant pairwise
   contrasts. It gives no per-task means: Figs. 10–11 pool across tasks.
   - 1D least-uncertain: S3 says "glyph colour ≈ surface colour > error bars", but the
     paper reports that error bars beat surface colour.
   - 1D most-uncertain: S3 says "glyph size > error bars", but no such difference is
     reported.
2. **Correll et al. 2018, E2 (High).** S3 ranks VSUPs as "best-performing" in the
   Battleship task. The paper found no effect on mean uncertainty of placements. VSUPs
   avoided the most uncertain cells (KS test), but their placements carried higher
   average danger, i.e. lower expected value. The earlier S3 audit's qualification ("not
   a universal decision advantage") is not reflected.
3. **Kale et al. E2 N = 150 (Moderate; Not verifiable).** The archived paper does not
   state the E2 sample. The figure comes only from the authors' external CSV, which is
   not archived, and the cell carries no source note.
4. **Greis et al. Data = M (Moderate; Not supported by the archive).** The archived paper
   never states whether the rainfall/forecast data were real or simulated. The M code
   rests on the dissertation, which is not archived.
5. **Wainer & Francolini N (Moderate).** S2 gives only "N: 16". Experiment 2, which S3
   ranks separately, used 8 returning participants. The S2 caption says separate values
   are shown when samples differ.
6. **Several S3 cells overstate a winner or tie.**
   - Correll & Gleicher E1 (Moderate): box plots tied bar charts on confidence.
   - Gschwandtner "E3b" (Moderate): centred error bars alone were significantly best.
   - Sterzik E2 (Moderate): the authors call texture, waves, contours, colour and hulls
     "similar", not ordered.
   - Sterzik E3 (Moderate): texture ties colour on SES, and colour wins on sticks.
   - Kusumastuti (Moderate): the table omits that E2 practice used the ellipse display.
   - Scholz (Moderate): "mean rank 3.35" is actually a preference score, where 4 means
     most preferred.
