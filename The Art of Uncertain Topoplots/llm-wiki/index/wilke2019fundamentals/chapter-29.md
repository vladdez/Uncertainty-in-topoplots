## Actionable principles

<a id="principle-29-sequence-figures-as-story"></a>

### Use figure sequence to create a factual question and resolution

**Problem addressed:** A single dense figure may reveal the answer without building understanding or may fail to connect observations into a point.

**Recommended solution:** Arrange figures so one establishes context or challenge and the next resolves it; compress to one figure only when the medium demands it.

**Why it works:** Ordered facts create a story arc that guides reasoning and memory without inventing evidence.

**Applies when:** Papers, talks, reports, and explanatory posts.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** A short-attention medium may require a single self-contained figure.

**Book example:** Figures 29-1 and 29-2 first show q-bio growth stopping and then reveal the coincident rise of bioRxiv.

**Search terms:** `story arc`, `figure sequence`, `challenge resolution`, `communication`

<a id="principle-29-simplify-to-main-point"></a>

### Remove data dimensions tangential to the intended message

**Problem addressed:** A technically sophisticated figure can bury the conclusion under variables the story does not need.

**Recommended solution:** State the audience and intended point, then keep only the encodings needed to see it quickly.

**Why it works:** Busy readers cannot spend long deciphering a chart; added dimensions divide attention and can leave no clear takeaway.

**Applies when:** Communicative figures after analysis is complete.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Exploratory figures may legitimately show more dimensions; supporting analyses can be shown separately.

**Book example:** Figures 29-3 to 29-5 replace an overloaded flight-delay bubble/trend plot with two simple bars answering the key questions.

**Search terms:** `simplification`, `main message`, `audience`, `extraneous dimension`

**Evidence:** passages [p355-b001](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p355-b001), [p355-b003](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p355-b003), [p356-b003](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p356-b003), [p357-b001](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p357-b001); figures [Figure 29-3](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p356-b002), [Figure 29-4](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p357-b002).

<a id="principle-29-stage-complexity"></a>

### Introduce a complex figure through a simple subset

**Problem addressed:** Showing a large multipanel display all at once overloads an audience unfamiliar with its grammar.

**Recommended solution:** First explain one representative panel or subset, then reveal the complete figure.

**Why it works:** The audience learns the encoding on a small example and can reuse that understanding across the larger display.

**Applies when:** Presentations and documents with complex small multiples.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** A familiar audience or simple figure may not need staged introduction.

**Book example:** Figures 29-6 and 29-7 move from one airline/airport weekday pattern to a grid covering ten airlines and three airports.

**Search terms:** `progressive disclosure`, `complex figure`, `small multiples`, `presentation`

**Evidence:** passages [p358-b001](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p358-b001); figures [Figure 29-6](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p358-b002), [Figure 29-7](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p359-b001).

<a id="principle-29-memorability-without-confusion"></a>

### Add distinctiveness without sacrificing quick comprehension

**Problem addressed:** Generic charts blur together, but decorative complexity can make exact reading difficult.

**Recommended solution:** Use a modest, subject-relevant visual device only after clarity is secured.

**Why it works:** Unique visuals improve memory, yet complexity also slows overview and comparison; the design must balance both effects.

**Applies when:** Public communication and sequences of similar figures.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** A memorable but confusing figure is not a successful data visualization.

**Book example:** Figures 29-8 and 29-9 compare a generic pet bar chart with an isotype design using repeated animal images.

**Search terms:** `memorability`, `isotype`, `distinctiveness`, `clarity`

**Evidence:** passages [p360-b001](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p360-b001), [p360-b003](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p360-b003); figures [Figure 29-8](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p360-b002), [Figure 29-9](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p361-b002).

<a id="principle-29-consistent-not-repetitive"></a>

### Keep semantics consistent while varying form across distinct analyses

**Problem addressed:** Identical chart forms can make different findings blur together, even when styling is consistent.

**Recommended solution:** Retain common colors, fonts, and meanings, but select different appropriate chart types for different analytical points.

**Why it works:** Consistency signals one story; variation helps the audience recognize transitions and remember separate results.

**Applies when:** Compound figures, papers, and presentation sequences.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Do not vary chart type merely for novelty; each form still has to suit its data and comparison.

**Book example:** Figures 29-11 and 29-12 replace a repetitive second stock time series with a bar chart of percent increase.

**Search terms:** `consistency`, `repetition`, `figure sequence`, `chart variety`

**Evidence:** passages [p362-b002](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p362-b002), [p363-b002](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p363-b002), [p364-b002](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p364-b002); figures [Figure 29-11](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p364-b001), [Figure 29-12](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p365-b001).

<a id="principle-29-raw-before-derived"></a>

### Show raw evidence before increasingly derived summaries

**Problem addressed:** A derived coefficient or percent change can be hard to trust or understand without seeing the observations it summarizes.

**Recommended solution:** Start a figure sequence close to the raw data, then introduce averages, changes, or model coefficients as needed.

**Why it works:** Raw views are more intuitive; derived views efficiently summarize the key trend after the audience has context.

**Applies when:** Multi-figure scientific narratives.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Showing every raw detail can become repetitive; the sequence should remain concise and medium-appropriate.

**Book example:** Figure 29-12 first shows Facebook's stock trajectory and then compares derived percentage increases across companies.

**Search terms:** `raw data`, `derived quantity`, `figure sequence`, `summary`

**Evidence:** passages [p365-b002](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p365-b002), [p366-b001](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p366-b001); figures [Figure 29-12](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p365-b001).
