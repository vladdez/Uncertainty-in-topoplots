## Actionable principles

<a id="principle-23-retain-enough-context"></a>

### Do not erase the context that anchors the data

**Problem addressed:** Excessive minimalism leaves marks floating, facets undefined, and legends confused with observations.

**Recommended solution:** Add only the framing, background, or guide lines needed to bind elements and support comparison.

**Why it works:** Non-data ink can establish baselines, group boundaries, and visual unity; removing all of it can reduce comprehension.

**Applies when:** Minimalist plots and small multiples.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Context must remain subordinate to the data; heavy grids can overpower foreground marks.

**Book example:** Figures 23-5 and 23-6 add light facet backgrounds and horizontal guides to a floating Titanic small-multiple display.

**Search terms:** `minimalism`, `context`, `floating marks`, `facet background`

**Evidence:** passages [p297-b001](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p297-b001), [p298-b001](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p298-b001); figures [Figure 23-5](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p298-b002), [Figure 23-6](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p299-b001).

<a id="principle-23-grid-perpendicular-to-comparison"></a>

### Use grid lines that support the primary comparison

**Problem addressed:** Full or strongly styled grids can compete with the data without aiding the task.

**Recommended solution:** Choose light guides oriented perpendicular to the key value dimension; omit unhelpful directions.

**Why it works:** Perpendicular guides provide reference points for reading mark positions or lengths.

**Applies when:** Line, bar, dot, and similar axis-based plots.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Scatterplots without a primary direction may benefit from a grid in both directions; visual branding can also affect background choice.

**Book example:** Figure 23-10 uses thin horizontal guides for stock values, while Figure 23-11 uses vertical guides for horizontal bars.

**Search terms:** `grid line`, `orientation`, `reference`, `visual hierarchy`

**Evidence:** passages [p301-b001](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p301-b001), [p303-b003](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p303-b003), [p307-b002](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p307-b002); figures [Figure 23-10](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p302-b002), [Figure 23-11](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p303-b002).

<a id="principle-23-use-task-specific-reference-line"></a>

### Use the reference line that directly encodes the comparison

**Problem addressed:** A generic grid can obscure deviation from a scientifically meaningful null or equality relation.

**Recommended solution:** Draw the relevant reference—such as x=y for paired data—and remove competing grid detail when it becomes busy.

**Why it works:** A task-specific line immediately separates above/below or positive/negative deviation.

**Applies when:** Paired scatterplots, residuals, thresholds, and null comparisons.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Combining a full grid and strong reference line may overwhelm the points; choose the smallest sufficient context.

**Book example:** Figures 23-13 to 23-15 compare a diagonal equality line, a generic grid, and an overly busy combination.

**Search terms:** `reference line`, `x equals y`, `paired data`, `null`

**Evidence:** passages [p304-b003](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p304-b003), [p306-b002](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p306-b002); figures [Figure 23-13](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p305-b001), [Figure 23-14](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p306-b001).
