## Actionable principles

<a id="principle-25-prefer-solid-shapes"></a>

### Prefer solid filled shapes to outline-only marks

**Problem addressed:** Outlines can make inside/outside ambiguous, create optical clutter, and weaken the sense of amount.

**Recommended solution:** Use restrained solid fills for bars, histograms, distributions, points, and boxes.

**Why it works:** Filled regions are perceived as coherent objects and convey area more immediately.

**Applies when:** Bars, histograms, density areas, points, and boxplots.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** The recommendation never overrides proportional ink; fills must still encode quantities faithfully.

**Book example:** Figures 25-1 and 25-2 contrast empty and filled Titanic histograms.

**Search terms:** `solid fill`, `outline`, `histogram`, `coherent object`

**Evidence:** passages [p314-b002](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p314-b002); figures [Figure 25-1](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p315-b001), [Figure 25-2](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p315-b002).

<a id="principle-25-transparent-fill-for-overlap"></a>

### Use transparent fills when distribution areas overlap

**Problem addressed:** Line-only densities have weak visual presence, while opaque fills hide distributions behind them.

**Recommended solution:** Fill density areas with partial transparency.

**Why it works:** Shading makes each distribution a distinct object while transparency keeps the others visible.

**Applies when:** A small number of overlapping density curves.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Many strongly overlapping distributions may still require faceting or another arrangement.

**Book example:** Figures 25-3 to 25-5 progress from dashed outlines to colored lines to transparent filled densities.

**Search terms:** `density fill`, `transparency`, `overlap`, `line drawing`

**Evidence:** passages [p316-b003](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p316-b003); figures [Figure 25-5](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p317-b002).

<a id="principle-25-solid-points-not-open"></a>

### Use solid points and solve overplotting directly

**Problem addressed:** Open and differently outlined symbols create visual noise and have weak presence.

**Recommended solution:** Use solid color-and-shape coding, and address overplotting with the methods from Chapter 18.

**Why it works:** Filled points separate groups more cleanly and remain usable in grayscale with redundant shape.

**Applies when:** Grouped scatterplots.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Wilke acknowledges the argument that open centers reveal covered points but judges the noise cost greater in general.

**Book example:** Figures 25-6 and 25-7 contrast black-and-white outline symbols with solid color-and-shape coding.

**Search terms:** `solid point`, `open symbol`, `overplotting`, `redundant shape`

**Evidence:** passages [p319-b002](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p319-b002); figures [Figure 25-6](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p318-b002), [Figure 25-7](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p319-b001).
