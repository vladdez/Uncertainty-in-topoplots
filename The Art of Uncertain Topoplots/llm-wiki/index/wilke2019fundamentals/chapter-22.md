## Actionable principles

<a id="principle-22-one-title-in-right-place"></a>

### Give every figure one title in the location its medium expects

**Problem addressed:** Missing, duplicated, or misplaced titles weaken the message and conflict with publication layout.

**Recommended solution:** State the figure's point once: in the caption block for conventional articles/books, or integrated into a standalone infographic.

**Why it works:** A title tells readers what the figure is about, while one authoritative location prevents redundancy and layout conflict.

**Applies when:** Manuscripts, reports, web graphics, and social media.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Integrated title/subtitle/source text is appropriate when the figure must stand alone without an external caption.

**Book example:** Figures 22-1 and 22-2 present the same corruption graphic as a publication figure and a standalone infographic.

**Search terms:** `title`, `caption`, `standalone infographic`, `publication`

**Evidence:** passages [p284-b003](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p284-b003), [p285-b002](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p285-b002); figures [Figure 22-1](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p285-b001), [Figure 22-2](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p286-b001).

<a id="principle-22-label-variables-and-units"></a>

### Label quantitative axes and legends with variables and units

**Problem addressed:** Readers cannot reliably infer what values mean or what measurement units apply.

**Recommended solution:** Name each mapped variable and give units for quantitative measures whenever possible.

**Why it works:** Explicit mapping removes guesswork and lets the display be interpreted independently of assumptions.

**Applies when:** Axes, size/color legends, and any quantitative scale.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Titles may be omitted when tick or category labels are fully self-explanatory, such as obvious calendar years.

**Book example:** Figure 22-3 labels blue-jay mass, length, skull size, and sex, including units for all numerical variables.

**Search terms:** `axis title`, `legend title`, `units`, `quantitative label`

**Evidence:** passages [p287-b001](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p287-b001), [p287-b003](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p287-b003); figures [Figure 22-3](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p287-b002).

<a id="principle-22-dont-make-readers-guess"></a>

### Omit labels only when meaning is genuinely obvious

**Problem addressed:** Contextual hints can leave readers nearly—but not completely—certain about an axis.

**Recommended solution:** Retain labels needed for unambiguous interpretation, while removing only clearly redundant titles.

**Why it works:** A caption rarely maps variables to axes as directly as a label does; uncertainty about meaning interrupts reading.

**Applies when:** Minimalist figure design.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Overlabeling also adds awkward redundancy, and whole axes may be omitted in inherently axis-free or qualitatively focused displays.

**Book example:** Figures 22-4 to 22-6 range from an acceptably omitted year title to an ambiguous y axis and then excessive labeling.

**Search terms:** `axis omission`, `ambiguity`, `minimalism`, `redundant label`

**Evidence:** passages [p288-b003](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p288-b003), [p289-b002](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p289-b002); figures [Figure 22-5](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p289-b001), [Figure 22-6](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p290-b001).
