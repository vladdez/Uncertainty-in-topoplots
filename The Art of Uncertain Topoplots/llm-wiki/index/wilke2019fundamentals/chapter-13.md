## Actionable principles

<a id="principle-13-lines-require-order"></a>

### Connect observations only when an independent variable supplies order

**Problem addressed:** Lines between unrelated points imply interpolation and adjacency that do not exist.

**Recommended solution:** Use lines for time or another naturally ordered independent variable; state that lines guide the eye when interpolation may be misconstrued.

**Why it works:** Connections visually encode predecessor/successor structure, not additional observations.

**Applies when:** Time series and dose–response curves.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Sparse, widely separated measurements make the implied interpolation more questionable; a caption qualification can help.

**Book example:** Figures 13-1 and 13-2 show bioRxiv monthly observations before and after ordered connections are added.

**Search terms:** `line graph`, `temporal order`, `interpolation`, `guide to eye`

**Evidence:** passages [p148-b002](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p148-b002), [p149-b002](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p149-b002), [p150-b002](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p150-b002); figures [Figure 13-2](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p150-b001).

<a id="principle-13-mark-density-controls-emphasis"></a>

### Use points, lines, and fills to control temporal emphasis

**Problem addressed:** Dense points add clutter, while filled areas can imply magnitude from zero.

**Recommended solution:** Drop points when the overall trend is primary and sampling is dense; fill under a curve only with a zero baseline.

**Why it works:** Removing points deemphasizes individual observations; a filled area strongly separates above from below and encodes value through height.

**Applies when:** Dense time series and area charts.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Keep points when observations or sampling locations matter; nonzero-baseline area fills are invalid.

**Book example:** Figures 13-3 and 13-4 progressively emphasize the bioRxiv trend, with the area chart retaining a zero baseline.

**Search terms:** `time series`, `points`, `area fill`, `zero baseline`, `trend`

**Evidence:** passages [p150-b002](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p150-b002), [p151-b002](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p151-b002); figures [Figure 13-3](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p151-b001), [Figure 13-4](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p152-b001).

<a id="principle-13-direct-label-multiple-lines"></a>

### Directly label multiple time-series lines when possible

**Problem addressed:** A separate legend forces repeated visual lookup and matching.

**Recommended solution:** Place series names near their lines and remove redundant point shapes or legends.

**Why it works:** Direct labels reduce cognitive transfer between the legend and data.

**Applies when:** A manageable number of line series with separable endpoints or paths.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Crowded or crossing series may not leave clean label locations; small multiples may then work better.

**Book example:** Figure 13-7 streamlines a three-series preprint chart by labeling lines directly.

**Search terms:** `direct labels`, `line chart`, `legend`, `cognitive load`

**Evidence:** passages [p154-b001](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p154-b001); figures [Figure 13-7](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p154-b002).

<a id="principle-13-connected-scatter-needs-direction"></a>

### Show direction and pace in connected scatterplots

**Problem addressed:** A connected path of two response variables can become an uninterpretable scribble.

**Recommended solution:** Add temporal markers plus direction cues such as color progression or arrows.

**Why it works:** Readers otherwise cannot recover which way the path runs or how quickly it moves.

**Applies when:** Connected scatterplots of two variables evolving together.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Separate line graphs are usually easier to read; connected scatterplots can be rewarding once readers understand the form.

**Book example:** Figure 13-11 is labeled bad because it omits the date markers and color shading used in the preceding version.

**Search terms:** `connected scatterplot`, `direction`, `time markers`, `pace`

**Evidence:** passages [p158-b001](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p158-b001); figures [Figure 13-11](../../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p158-b002).
