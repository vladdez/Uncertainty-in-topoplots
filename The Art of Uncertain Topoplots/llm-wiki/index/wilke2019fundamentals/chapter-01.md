## Actionable principles

<a id="principle-01-integrity-before-decoration"></a>

### Preserve the data before improving appearance

**Problem addressed:** A visually attractive figure can still distort magnitudes or otherwise misstate the data.

**Recommended solution:** Require accurate, non-misleading encoding first; then make aesthetic choices that support rather than distract from the message.

**Why it works:** Wilke treats visualization as both science and art: incorrect magnitudes make a figure wrong, while distracting colors, imbalance, or typography make correct interpretation harder.

**Applies when:** Any scientific figure, especially when a redesign changes scales, geometry, or emphasis.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Aesthetic judgments retain subjectivity; accuracy and non-distortion do not.

**Book example:** Figure 1-1 contrasts a reasonable bar chart with an ugly version, a misleading version with separate scales, and a mathematically wrong version.

<a id="principle-01-triage-failure-type"></a>

### Classify a defect before fixing it

**Problem addressed:** Cosmetic, perceptual, and mathematical failures can be conflated, leading reviewers to prioritize the wrong change.

**Recommended solution:** Distinguish ugly (aesthetic), bad (perceptually unclear or deceptive), and wrong (mathematically incorrect) figures.

**Why it works:** The categories separate taste-sensitive concerns from failures that impede perception and from objective data errors.

**Applies when:** Initial review and prioritization of proposed changes.

**Exceptions and qualifications:** Wilke notes that the ugly/bad boundary is fluid and that some ratings are matters of taste.

**Book example:** In Figure 1-1, bright colors and mixed fonts are ugly, nonaligned per-bar scales are bad, and bars inconsistent with their stated values are wrong.
