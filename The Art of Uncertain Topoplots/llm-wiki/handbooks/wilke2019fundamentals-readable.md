| Principle | Problem | Recommended solution | Why it works | Applies when | Exceptions / qualifications | Book example | Source references |

| **Use equal-prominence colors for unordered categories**<br><small>qualitative palette, unordered categories, equal prominence</small> | A lightness sequence or one standout hue falsely suggests order or importance among nominal groups. | Choose a finite qualitative palette whose colors are distinguishable but approximately equal in visual prominence. | The palette should separate groups without creating an unintended ranking or focal category. | Countries, manufacturers, conditions, or other unordered categorical groups. | If one group is intentionally highlighted, use an accent strategy instead of pretending all categories are equivalent. | Figure 4-1 shows Okabe–Ito, ColorBrewer Dark2, and ggplot2 hue qualitative scales. | [Passage p044-b003](../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p044-b003)<br>[Figure 4-1](../raw/wilke2019fundamentals.md#p045-b001) |
