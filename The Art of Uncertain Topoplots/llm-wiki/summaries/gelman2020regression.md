# Regression and Other Stories

**Citation:** Andrew Gelman, Jennifer Hill, and Aki Vehtari (2021)  
**Topics:** `statistical methodology`, `uncertainty quantification`, `scientific reporting`  
**Drill down:** [paper index](../index/gelman2020regression.md) · [full extracted text](../raw/gelman2020regression.md)

## Why this paper matters

Applied handbook on regression as a tool for comparison, estimation, prediction, and causal inference. For literature-review work it is most useful for separating estimates from uncertainty, avoiding significance dichotomies, distinguishing association from causation, and checking fitted models graphically and through simulation.

## Key findings

- Model-based standard errors do not capture every source of uncertainty; bias, measurement, sampling, and modeling assumptions require separate consideration.
- Regression coefficients describe conditional comparisons and should not automatically be called causal effects.
- Posterior predictive checks compare observed data with replications from a fitted model to reveal discrepancies.

## Scope and cautions

- A broad methods textbook rather than a primary empirical study; use it for methodological framing and consult domain-specific primary evidence for substantive claims.
