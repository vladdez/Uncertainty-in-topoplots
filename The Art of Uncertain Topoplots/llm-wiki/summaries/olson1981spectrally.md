# Spectrally Encoded Two-Variable Maps

**Citation:** Olson, Judy M. (1981)  
**Topics:** `bivariate color maps`, `geospatial visualization`, `empirical evaluation`  
**Drill down:** [paper index](../index/olson1981spectrally.md) · [full extracted text](../raw/olson1981spectrally.md)

## Why this paper matters

Evaluates spectrally encoded two-variable maps through four experiments on color ordering, spatial-correlation judgment, open-ended information extraction, and reader attitudes. The results expose an initial interpretation hurdle but also show that successful readers can extract substantive information, especially when composite maps are paired with separate maps and explanatory notes.

## Key findings

- Participants did not spontaneously agree on the intended two-dimensional color ordering, although 62% preferred it after it was shown.
- Composite maps produced more spatial-correlation error overall, but successful composite-map readers could perform competitively.
- Explanatory notes improved attitudes, and the author recommends presenting composite and separate maps together.

## Scope and cautions

- The article states 466 participants overall, while the reported experiment-level counts sum to 465; the historical designs, samples, and tasks limit direct transfer to current displays.
