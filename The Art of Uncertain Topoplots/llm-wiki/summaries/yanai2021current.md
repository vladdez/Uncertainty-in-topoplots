# The Current State of Uncertainty Reporting in Ecosystem Studies: A Systematic Evaluation of Peer-Reviewed Literature

**Citation:** Yanai, Ruth D. and Mann, Thomas A. and Hong, Sunghoon D. and Pu, Ge and Zukswert, Jenna M. (2021)  
**Topics:** `scientific reporting`, `climate and ecology`, `uncertainty visualization`  
**Drill down:** [paper index](../index/yanai2021current.md) · [full extracted text](../raw/yanai2021current.md)

## Why this paper matters

Audits 132 ecosystem papers from 2019 for reporting of uncertainty in vegetation, soils, precipitation, and surface water. Most papers reported only a minority of relevant sources, with large differences by topic and stronger reporting when a topic was central to the paper.

## Key findings

- Average coverage of relevant uncertainty sources ranged from 21% for precipitation to 47% for surface water.
- Only 21% of papers made all data available and another 21% made some available after accounting for broken links.

## Scope and cautions

- Coding relevance was sometimes impossible when papers did not mention missing data, detection limits, or other practices.
