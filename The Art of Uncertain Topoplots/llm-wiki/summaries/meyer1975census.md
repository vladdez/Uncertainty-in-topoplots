# Color Statistical Mapping by the U.S. Bureau of the Census

**Citation:** Meyer, Morton A. and Broome, Frederick R. and Schweitzer, Jr., Richard H. (1975)  
**Topics:** `bivariate color maps`, `geospatial visualization`, `visualization history`  
**Drill down:** [paper index](../index/meyer1975census.md) · [full extracted text](../raw/meyer1975census.md)

## Why this paper matters

Documents the U.S. Census Bureau's color statistical mapping program and the operational origin of its two-variable choropleth cross-maps. The paper describes examples and production methods, presents anticipated analytical benefits, and states that reader evaluation was still underway rather than reporting validated perceptual performance.

## Key findings

- The Bureau director suggested crossing two choropleth maps in 1974, and the first cross-map combined maps of older Americans in 1970 and year of maximum county population.
- The authors presented cross-maps as a way to expose spatial relationships but reported that evaluation of class intervals, colors, and class counts was still underway.

## Scope and cautions

- A descriptive technical account with worked maps, not a controlled reader study; claimed interpretive benefits should be treated as author rationale rather than empirical findings.
