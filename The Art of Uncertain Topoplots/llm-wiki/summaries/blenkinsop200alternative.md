# Evaluating the Perception of Uncertainty in Alternative Visualization Strategies

**Citation:** Blenkinsop, Steve and Fisher, Pete and Bastin, Lucy and Wood, Jo (2000)  
**Topics:** `uncertainty visualization`, `geospatial visualization`, `empirical evaluation`  
**Drill down:** [paper index](../index/blenkinsop200alternative.md) · [full extracted text](../raw/blenkinsop200alternative.md)

## Why this paper matters

Compares greyscale, random animation, serial animation, and linked ancillary views for communicating uncertainty in fuzzy land-cover classifications. A structured test with experienced GIS graduates and novice students shows that performance depends strongly on the judgment task and on participant experience.

## Key findings

- A greyscale legend substantially improved uncertainty interpretation in both experience groups.
- Random animation supported relative comparisons better than absolute estimates, while linked histograms improved some judgments beyond animation alone.
- The authors rank greyscale as most effective overall, but emphasize task- and expertise-dependent performance.

## Scope and cautions

- Small purposive samples, untimed performance, one fuzzy remote-sensing setting, and no test of animation speed or color limit generalization.
