# Error Bars Considered Harmful: Exploring Alternate Encodings for Mean and Error

**Citation:** Correll, Michael and Gleicher, Michael (2014)  
**Topics:** `uncertainty visualization`, `empirical evaluation`, `statistical graphics`  
**Drill down:** [paper index](../index/correll2014error.md) · [full extracted text](../raw/correll2014error.md)

## Why this paper matters

Crowdsourced experiments compare conventional error bars with distributional alternatives, showing that encoding choices affect judgments and confidence and that bar-like containment can invite biased probability interpretations.

## Key findings

- Bar-based displays produced a within-the-bar bias that symmetric distributional encodings mitigated.
- Display form affected confidence and inferential strategy, so equivalent statistics are not perceptually interchangeable.

## Scope and cautions

- The tasks use stylized statistical displays and online participants; the two alternatives were not consistently distinguishable in performance.
