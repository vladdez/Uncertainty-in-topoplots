# Value-Suppressing Uncertainty Palettes

**Citation:** Correll, Michael and Moritz, Dominik and Heer, Jeffrey (2018)  
**Topics:** `uncertainty visualization`, `bivariate color maps`, `empirical evaluation`  
**Drill down:** [paper index](../index/correll2018task.md) · [full extracted text](../raw/correll2018task.md)

## Why this paper matters

Introduces value-suppressing uncertainty palettes (VSUPs), which allocate fewer distinguishable value bins as uncertainty grows. A crowdsourced evaluation compares these palettes with conventional bivariate and juxtaposed maps for identification and decision tasks.

## Key findings

- VSUPs caused participants to weight uncertainty more strongly and avoid highly uncertain choices.
- Superimposed maps outperformed juxtaposed maps in the identification task, and discrete bins outperformed continuous color for precise identification.

## Scope and cautions

- Results depend on the experimental tasks, risk framing, and chosen palette construction.
