# Hypothetical Outcome Plots Outperform Error Bars and Violin Plots for Inferences about Reliability of Variable Ordering

**Citation:** Hullman, Jessica and Resnick, Paul and Adar, Eytan (2015)  
**Topics:** `uncertainty visualization`, `statistical graphics`, `empirical evaluation`  
**Drill down:** [paper index](../index/hullman2015hypothetical.md) · [full extracted text](../raw/hullman2015hypothetical.md)

## Why this paper matters

Introduces hypothetical outcome plots (HOPs), animations of sampled outcomes intended to let viewers reason through counting and visual integration. In a 288-person experiment, HOPs substantially improved judgments about the ordering of two or three uncertain quantities compared with error bars and violin plots.

## Key findings

- HOPs yielded much lower error for two-variable ordering-probability judgments.
- For many single-variable questions, accuracy was similar across HOPs, error bars, and violin plots; HOPs were not uniformly superior.

## Scope and cautions

- Animated displays can require more viewing time and the study used specific distributional tasks with online participants.
