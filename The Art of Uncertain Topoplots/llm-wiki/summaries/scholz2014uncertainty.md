# Uncertainty in Geographic Data on Bivariate Maps: An Examination of Visualization Preference and Decision Making

**Citation:** Scholz, Ruojing W. and Lu, Yongmei (2014)  
**Topics:** `uncertainty visualization`, `bivariate maps`, `decision making`  
**Drill down:** [paper index](../index/scholz2014uncertainty.md) · [full extracted text](../raw/scholz2014uncertainty.md)

## Why this paper matters

A survey experiment compares four bivariate map encodings of geographic uncertainty, examining preferences and a decision task among college students with differing GIS knowledge.

## Key findings

- Fuzziness and lightness ranked above transparency and shape among the 37 valid preference rankings.
- GIS knowledge helped performance, while preference alone did not establish decision effectiveness.

## Scope and cautions

- Only 37 complete rankings supported the preference comparison, and the student sample and scenario constrain generalization.
