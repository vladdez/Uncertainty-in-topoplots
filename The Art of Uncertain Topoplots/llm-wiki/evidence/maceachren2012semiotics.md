## Research question

- Which abstract and iconic symbols are intuitive for distinct uncertainty components, and which support aggregate map judgments? **Evidence:** [PDF p. 2](../raw/maceachren2012semiotics.md#p002-b002) (passage); [PDF p. 3](../raw/maceachren2012semiotics.md#p003-b003) (passage); [PDF p. 7](../raw/maceachren2012semiotics.md#p007-b003) (passage)

## Claim-level evidence

<a id="claim-maceachren-intuitiveness"></a>

### maceachren-intuitiveness

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** Fuzziness, location, and value were rated most intuitive for ordinal discrete-entity uncertainty, while arrangement, size, and transparency were acceptable but weaker.  
**Quantitative detail:** Top variables had means above 5 on a 1–7 scale and modal ratings of 7; acceptable variables had means 4–5.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 4](../raw/maceachren2012semiotics.md#p004-b002) (passage)
<a id="claim-maceachren-aggregate"></a>

### maceachren-aggregate

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** Effectiveness for aggregate uncertainty varied across uncertainty conditions and symbol sets, so intuitive isolated symbols were not universally optimal in map tasks.  
**Quantitative detail:** Experiment 2 N=30; 20 symbol sets × 12 configurations = 240 trials per participant.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 7](../raw/maceachren2012semiotics.md#p007-b003) (passage); [Table 4, PDF p. 8](../raw/maceachren2012semiotics.md#p008-b002) (table); [Table 5, PDF p. 8](../raw/maceachren2012semiotics.md#p008-b003) (table)


## Conclusions

- Uncertainty symbol design should reflect what kind of uncertainty is represented and should be tested in the actual multi-symbol task, not only rated in isolation. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 8](../raw/maceachren2012semiotics.md#p008-b004) (passage)

## Limitations

- Purposeful GIS-aware samples and abstract map configurations constrain transfer to novices, continuous fields, and EEG topoplots. **Provenance:** `wiki_scope_note`. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 3](../raw/maceachren2012semiotics.md#p003-b003) (passage); [PDF p. 7](../raw/maceachren2012semiotics.md#p007-b003) (passage)
