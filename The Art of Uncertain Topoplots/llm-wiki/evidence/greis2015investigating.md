## Research question

- How do non-experts judge alternative weather-uncertainty representations, and do those judgments predict decisions? **Evidence:** [PDF p. 286](../raw/greis2015investigating.md#p286-b004) (passage); [PDF p. 287](../raw/greis2015investigating.md#p287-b002) (passage)

## Contributions

- Separates subjective ratings of twelve representations from behavior with four representations in a farming decision game. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 289](../raw/greis2015investigating.md#p289-b002) (passage); [PDF p. 292](../raw/greis2015investigating.md#p292-b002) (passage)

## Methods

| Component | Extracted detail |
| --- | --- |
| Design | Random-order online survey followed by a controlled turn-based game. |
| Sample | Survey N=90 (36 women, 54 men; ages 18–82, mean 31, SD 12.6); experiment N=12 (4 women, 8 men). |
| Conditions | Twelve textual and graphical forecast representations in the survey; four selected representations over ten game rounds each. |
| Measurements | Five-level ratings, forecast use, decisions, and final display preference. |
| Analysis | Representation comparisons and descriptive behavioral outcomes. |
| Evidence | [PDF p. 289](../raw/greis2015investigating.md#p289-b002) (passage); [PDF p. 290](../raw/greis2015investigating.md#p290-b003) (passage); [PDF p. 292](../raw/greis2015investigating.md#p292-b002) (passage) |

## Claim-level evidence

<a id="claim-greis-more-not-better"></a>

### greis-more-not-better

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** Providing more uncertainty information did not automatically yield better judgments or choices.  
**Quantitative detail:** Survey N=90; game N=12 and 442 forecast-opened rounds analyzed from 480 total rounds.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 286](../raw/greis2015investigating.md#p286-b004) (passage); [PDF p. 292](../raw/greis2015investigating.md#p292-b002) (passage)
<a id="claim-greis-preference-performance"></a>

### greis-preference-performance

**Type:** `author_interpretation`  
**Claim:** Preference and survey appraisal did not consistently predict decision performance in the game.  
**Quantitative detail:** Eight of 12 game participants preferred the probability-distribution representation.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 292](../raw/greis2015investigating.md#p292-b002) (passage); [PDF p. 292](../raw/greis2015investigating.md#p292-b004) (passage)


## Conclusions

- Uncertainty representations for non-experts should be evaluated through actual decisions as well as ratings, because additional detail may alienate rather than help. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 292](../raw/greis2015investigating.md#p292-b004) (passage)
