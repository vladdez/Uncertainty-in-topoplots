## Research question

- Do hypothetical outcome plots support more accurate judgments about distributions and uncertain ordering than error bars or violin plots? **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/hullman2015hypothetical.md#p001-b004) (passage); [PDF p. 5](../raw/hullman2015hypothetical.md#p005-b003) (passage)

## Contributions

- Introduces animated sampled-outcome plots and experimentally compares them with two static distribution representations across one-, two-, and three-variable inference tasks. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/hullman2015hypothetical.md#p001-b004) (passage); [PDF p. 5](../raw/hullman2015hypothetical.md#p005-b003) (passage)

## Methods

| Component | Extracted detail |
| --- | --- |
| Design | Between-participant Amazon Mechanical Turk experiment. |
| Sample | 288 U.S. participants after the study's quality-control procedures. |
| Conditions | Each participant saw HOPs, error bars, or violin plots and completed nine tasks involving one, two, or three normally distributed quantities. |
| Measurements | Absolute error in reported distribution properties and completion time; interaction with HOP animation was logged. |
| Analysis | Between-condition error comparisons and timing analyses, including ANOVA. |
| Evidence | [PDF p. 5](../raw/hullman2015hypothetical.md#p005-b003) (passage); [PDF p. 12](../raw/hullman2015hypothetical.md#p012-b002) (passage) |

## Claim-level evidence

<a id="claim-hullman-hop-ordering"></a>

### hullman-hop-ordering

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** HOPs produced much more accurate judgments for two- and three-quantity ordering tasks and broadly comparable performance for most one-variable questions.  
**Quantitative detail:** N=288; detailed task-specific errors are reported in the study results and figures.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/hullman2015hypothetical.md#p001-b004) (passage); [PDF p. 13](../raw/hullman2015hypothetical.md#p013-b005) (passage)
<a id="claim-hullman-hop-time"></a>

### hullman-hop-time

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** HOP participants took somewhat longer on average, but the overall completion-time difference was not statistically significant.  
**Quantitative detail:** Means 523 s (HOP), 479 s (error bars), 481 s (violin); F(2,285)=2.02, p>.1.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 12](../raw/hullman2015hypothetical.md#p012-b002) (passage)
<a id="claim-hullman-frequency-account"></a>

### hullman-frequency-account

**Type:** `author_interpretation`  
**Claim:** The authors argue that HOPs can support reasoning through counting and visual integration while representing joint distributions more expressively than marginal error bars or violins.  
**Quantitative detail:** Not applicable.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 23](../raw/hullman2015hypothetical.md#p023-b003) (passage)


## Conclusions

- Sample-based animation is promising for joint-ordering inferences, but it is not uniformly superior for every distributional question. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 15](../raw/hullman2015hypothetical.md#p015-b004) (passage); [PDF p. 23](../raw/hullman2015hypothetical.md#p023-b003) (passage)

## Limitations

- The authors knew little about the Mechanical Turk sample beyond screening; tasks used independent normal distributions, and performance may differ for other audiences, dependence structures, or HOP speeds and sample counts. **Provenance:** `authors`. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 18](../raw/hullman2015hypothetical.md#p018-b003) (passage); [PDF p. 20](../raw/hullman2015hypothetical.md#p020-b002) (passage)
