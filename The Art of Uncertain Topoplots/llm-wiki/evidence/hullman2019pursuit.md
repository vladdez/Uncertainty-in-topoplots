## Research question

- How has uncertainty visualization been evaluated, and which links from uncertain inputs to judgments, decisions, and outcomes remain understudied? **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/hullman2019pursuit.md#p001-b003) (passage); [PDF p. 1](../raw/hullman2019pursuit.md#p001-b004) (passage)

## Contributions

- Develops a six-level evaluation taxonomy and codes complete evaluation paths through 86 publications. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/hullman2019pursuit.md#p001-b003) (passage); [PDF p. 4](../raw/hullman2019pursuit.md#p004-b002) (passage)

### hullman-confirmation-bias

**Type:** `author_interpretation`  
**Claim:** Published evaluations emphasize confirmatory accuracy with constrained inputs more than explanation and downstream decision quality.  
**Quantitative detail:** Approximately 20% (72 paths) used confidence-related constructs.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 7](../raw/hullman2019pursuit.md#p007-b003) (passage); [PDF p. 9](../raw/hullman2019pursuit.md#p009-b002) (passage)


## Conclusions

- Evaluation should trace how displays affect judgments and decisions rather than stop at low-level value reading or preference. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 9](../raw/hullman2019pursuit.md#p009-b002) (passage)

## Limitations

- The review ends in 2018 and heterogeneity of constructs limits direct effect comparison; publication frequency is not design efficacy. **Provenance:** `wiki_scope_note`. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/hullman2019pursuit.md#p001-b004) (passage); [PDF p. 4](../raw/hullman2019pursuit.md#p004-b002) (passage)
