# Research evidence: Thematic Uncertainty Visualization Usability: Comparison of Basic Methods

**Source type:** comparative usability study

## Research question

- How usable are separate versus combined thematic uncertainty maps for decoding predicted values, uncertainty, and both together? **Evidence:** [PDF p. 4](../raw/kubicek2011thematic.md#p004-b002) (passage)

## Contributions

- Compares maps-compared and whitening-based maps-combined designs at multiple task levels while considering intuitiveness, serial versus parallel processing, and user background. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 4](../raw/kubicek2011thematic.md#p004-b002) (passage); [PDF p. 6](../raw/kubicek2011thematic.md#p006-b003) (passage)

## Methods

| Component | Extracted detail |
| --- | --- |
| Design | Laboratory/web-based task comparison across three participant groups. |
| Sample | 104 participants total (63 men, 41 women): two homogeneous student groups and a heterogeneous open-day group of 50 aged 15–61. |
| Conditions | Separate maps versus combined whitening; tasks tested uncertainty intuitiveness, value decoding, uncertainty decoding, and joint value-plus-uncertainty decoding. |
| Measurements | Accuracy, decision time, preferences, and demographic/background variables. |
| Analysis | Group and technique comparisons with significance tests; some specialist/non-specialist comparisons were intentionally avoided because groups differed. |
| Evidence | [PDF p. 6](../raw/kubicek2011thematic.md#p006-b003) (passage); [PDF p. 9](../raw/kubicek2011thematic.md#p009-b015) (passage) |

## Claim-level evidence

<a id="claim-kubicek-intuitiveness"></a>

### kubicek-intuitiveness

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** Sixty-three percent associated lighter appearance with higher uncertainty and those participants decided faster in the intuitiveness task.  
**Quantitative detail:** 63% of participants.  
**Evidence:** [Figure 4, PDF p. 8](../raw/kubicek2011thematic.md#p008-b013) (figure)
<a id="claim-kubicek-speed-accuracy"></a>

### kubicek-speed-accuracy

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** Separate maps were slower but more accurate than the combined whitening display in the reported joint-processing comparison.  
**Quantitative detail:** Separate/serial: 67 of 104 correct (64%); combined/parallel: 59 of 104 (56%).  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 10](../raw/kubicek2011thematic.md#p010-b002) (passage)

### kubicek-preliminary

**Type:** `author_interpretation`  
**Claim:** The authors treat the results as preliminary and stress that apparent intuitiveness can depend on task order and prior exposure.  
**Quantitative detail:** Not applicable.

## Conclusions

- Neither separate nor combined mapping dominates across all tasks; joint decoding was the most problematic and warrants further cognitive testing. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 10](../raw/kubicek2011thematic.md#p010-b002) (passage); [PDF p. 10](../raw/kubicek2011thematic.md#p010-b005) (passage)

## Limitations

- Groups differed in age and expertise, some comparisons were therefore avoided, task order may have favored whitening, and the authors call the findings preliminary. **Provenance:** `authors`. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 7](../raw/kubicek2011thematic.md#p007-b003) (passage); [PDF p. 9](../raw/kubicek2011thematic.md#p009-b015) (passage); [PDF p. 10](../raw/kubicek2011thematic.md#p010-b005) (passage)
