## Research question

- How completely do ecosystem studies report relevant sources of uncertainty and make their data available? **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/yanai2021current.md#p001-b004) (passage)

## Contributions

- Applies a source-specific coding framework to randomly sampled ecosystem papers across vegetation, soils, precipitation, and surface water. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/yanai2021current.md#p001-b004) (passage); [PDF p. 10](../raw/yanai2021current.md#p010-b003) (passage)

## Methods

| Component | Extracted detail |
| --- | --- |
| Design | Systematic audit of randomly selected published papers. |
| Sample | 132 papers published in 2019, sampled from 100 scientific journals. |
| Conditions | Each paper coded for applicable uncertainty sources in four ecosystem topic areas and for data availability. |
| Measurements | Fraction of relevant uncertainty sources reported and availability of all or some data. |
| Analysis | Descriptive rates and comparisons by topic and uncertainty source. |
| Evidence | [PDF p. 1](../raw/yanai2021current.md#p001-b004) (passage); [PDF p. 9](../raw/yanai2021current.md#p009-b003) (passage) |

## Claim-level evidence

<a id="claim-yanai-reporting-rates"></a>

### yanai-reporting-rates

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** Most papers reported only a minority of uncertainty sources judged relevant, with substantial differences across ecosystem topics.  
**Quantitative detail:** Average coverage: surface water 47%±5%, soils 45%±4%, vegetation 32%; overall 54% of papers quantified fewer than half of applicable sources, mean score 42%, median 40%.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/yanai2021current.md#p001-b004) (passage); [PDF p. 11](../raw/yanai2021current.md#p011-b002) (passage)
<a id="claim-yanai-data-availability"></a>

### yanai-data-availability

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** Broken links reduced effective data availability below nominal reporting rates.  
**Quantitative detail:** 24% linked all data and 48% at least some; after eight ineffective links, 21% provided all data and 42% at least some.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 9](../raw/yanai2021current.md#p009-b003) (passage); [Fig. 7, PDF p. 10](../raw/yanai2021current.md#p010-b004) (figure)
<a id="claim-yanai-sampling-error"></a>

### yanai-sampling-error

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** Sampling error was the most commonly reported uncertainty source across the assessed disciplines, while other relevant sources were often omitted.  
**Quantitative detail:** Selected categories exceeded 80%; other source/topic combinations were much lower.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 10](../raw/yanai2021current.md#p010-b003) (passage)


## Conclusions

- Uncertainty reporting in ecosystem studies is incomplete and uneven, limiting evaluation and reuse of published conclusions. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 11](../raw/yanai2021current.md#p011-b002) (passage)

## Limitations

- Applicability coding was sometimes impossible when articles omitted information about missing data, detection limits, or handling practices; the sample is restricted to 2019 ecosystem literature. **Provenance:** `authors`. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/yanai2021current.md#p001-b004) (passage); [PDF p. 9](../raw/yanai2021current.md#p009-b003) (passage)
