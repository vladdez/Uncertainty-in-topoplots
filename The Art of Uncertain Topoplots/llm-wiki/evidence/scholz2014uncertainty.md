## Research question

- How do readers prefer and use fuzziness, lightness, transparency, and shape to represent uncertainty on bivariate geographic maps? **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/scholz2014uncertainty.md#p001-b007) (passage); [PDF p. 4](../raw/scholz2014uncertainty.md#p004-b002) (passage)

## Contributions

## Methods

| Component | Extracted detail |

## Claim-level evidence

<a id="claim-scholz-preference"></a>

### scholz-preference

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** Among valid rankings, fuzziness and lightness were preferred over transparency and shape.  
**Quantitative detail:** N=37 valid rankings; mean ranks 3.35, 2.97, 2.38, and 1.30 respectively; agreement tests p≤.005.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 7](../raw/scholz2014uncertainty.md#p007-b004) (passage)
<a id="claim-scholz-decision"></a>

### scholz-decision

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** Most participants incorporated displayed uncertainty into the experimental choice rather than treating the mapped estimates as equal.  
**Quantitative detail:** Approximately 82% chose equality without uncertainty; about 70% selected option b when uncertainty was shown.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 8](../raw/scholz2014uncertainty.md#p008-b005) (passage)


## Conclusions

- Fuzziness and lightness were promising in the tested setting, and GIS knowledge aided use, but preference should not be equated with universal effectiveness. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 10](../raw/scholz2014uncertainty.md#p010-b003) (passage)

## Limitations

- Incomplete rankings, a student sample, and one geographic decision scenario restrict inference; the task-defined correct use of uncertainty is not universal decision evidence. **Provenance:** `wiki_scope_note`. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 7](../raw/scholz2014uncertainty.md#p007-b004) (passage); [PDF p. 8](../raw/scholz2014uncertainty.md#p008-b005) (passage)
