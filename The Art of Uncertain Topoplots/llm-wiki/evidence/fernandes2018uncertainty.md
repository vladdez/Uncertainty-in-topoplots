# Research evidence: Uncertainty Displays Using Quantile Dotplots or CDFs Improve Transit Decision-Making

**Source type:** preregistered incentivized online experiment  
**Evidence topics:** `quantile dotplots`, `CDFs`, `decision quality`

## Research question

- Which uncertainty displays help people make high-quality, consistent transit decisions and learn an effective policy over repeated trials? **Evidence:** [PDF p. 6](../raw/fernandes2018uncertainty.md#p006-b002) (passage)

## Contributions

- Evaluates ten uncertainty displays with a realistic bus-arrival distribution, explicit utilities, and optimal-payoff benchmarks. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 2](../raw/fernandes2018uncertainty.md#p002-b002) (passage); [PDF p. 4](../raw/fernandes2018uncertainty.md#p004-b003) (passage)

## Methods

| Component | Extracted detail |
| --- | --- |
| Design | Between-condition online experiment with repeated incentivized decisions. |
| Sample | 408 participants in the final analysis; 385 supplied complete demographics; more than 80 additional participants were used in pilots. |
| Conditions | Ten displays including quantile dotplots, CDFs, PDFs, intervals, text, and a control across 40 trials. |
| Measurements | Expected payoff and payoff relative to an optimal strategy, plus response consistency and learning across trials. |
| Analysis | Preregistered Bayesian mixed-effects beta regression with participant intercepts and slopes and scenario intercepts. |
| Evidence | [PDF p. 7](../raw/fernandes2018uncertainty.md#p007-b002) (passage); [PDF p. 7](../raw/fernandes2018uncertainty.md#p007-b003) (passage); [PDF p. 8](../raw/fernandes2018uncertainty.md#p008-b002) (passage) |

## Claim-level evidence

<a id="claim-fernandes-decision-quality"></a>

### fernandes-decision-quality

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** Quantile dotplots and CDFs supported decisions closest to the optimal policy by the final trial.  
**Quantitative detail:** Mean final-trial performance was about 95% of optimal; for dot50, over 95% of predicted final-trial decisions exceeded 80% of optimal.  
**Evidence:** [Figure 4, PDF p. 9](../raw/fernandes2018uncertainty.md#p009-b011) (figure)
<a id="claim-fernandes-consistency"></a>

### fernandes-consistency

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** The strongest displays also produced more consistent decisions rather than only a higher average.  
**Quantitative detail:** Dot50 final-trial SD was about four percentage points lower than PDF, PDF-interval, text85, and control conditions.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 8](../raw/fernandes2018uncertainty.md#p008-b002) (passage)


## Conclusions

- Displays that support accurate probability-interval estimation can improve consequential decisions and learning in the tested transit context. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 9](../raw/fernandes2018uncertainty.md#p009-b011) (passage); [PDF p. 10](../raw/fernandes2018uncertainty.md#p010-b004) (passage)

## Limitations

- Online participants did not face an actual on-the-go context, and one experimenter-defined utility function cannot represent all riders or situations. **Provenance:** `authors`. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 10](../raw/fernandes2018uncertainty.md#p010-b004) (passage)
