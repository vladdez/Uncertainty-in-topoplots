## Research question

- Why do visualization authors omit uncertainty even when they acknowledge its value? **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/hullman2020why.md#p001-b002) (passage)

## Contributions

- Characterizes practitioners' values, rationales, and tensions around uncertainty communication and proposes research directions better aligned with author workflows and inference models. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/hullman2020why.md#p001-b002) (passage); [PDF p. 9](../raw/hullman2020why.md#p009-b001) (passage)

## Methods

| Component | Extracted detail |
| --- | --- |
| Design | Qualitative mixed-methods inquiry using an online survey and semi-structured interviews with open coding. |
| Sample | 90 authors who regularly create visualizations for others and 13 influential visualization designers; 11 interviews were professionally transcribed and two were not recorded. |
| Conditions | Questions concerned definitions, practices, audience assumptions, benefits, and reasons for omission. |
| Measurements | Coded themes and selected descriptive response frequencies. |
| Analysis | Open coding and interpretive thematic synthesis; recruitment stopped when rationales appeared to converge. |
| Evidence | [PDF p. 1](../raw/hullman2020why.md#p001-b002) (passage); [PDF p. 2](../raw/hullman2020why.md#p002-b001) (passage); [PDF p. 3](../raw/hullman2020why.md#p003-b001) (passage) |

## Conclusions

- Improving uncertainty communication requires addressing practitioners' real constraints and implicit inference models, not only inventing additional encodings. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 9](../raw/hullman2020why.md#p009-b001) (passage)

## Limitations

- Convenience and purposive samples do not estimate population prevalence; the author presents this as an initial inquiry and stopped recruitment after perceived thematic convergence. **Provenance:** `authors`. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 2](../raw/hullman2020why.md#p002-b001) (passage)
