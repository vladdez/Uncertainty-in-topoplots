## Research question

- Do hypothetical outcome plots help untrained observers infer which uncertain trend generated ambiguous data compared with error bars and line ensembles? **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/kale2019hypothetical.md#p001-b004) (passage)

## Contributions

- Uses psychometric functions to compare trend-discrimination thresholds and confidence under sample-based and static uncertainty displays. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/kale2019hypothetical.md#p001-b003) (passage); [PDF p. 4](../raw/kale2019hypothetical.md#p004-b002) (passage)

## Methods

| Component | Extracted detail |
| --- | --- |
| Design | Two within-participant MTurk experiments using adaptive evidence levels. |
| Sample | Experiment 1 recruited 62 MTurk Masters with a target of 50 usable participants; consult Experiment 2 methods for its final inclusion denominator. |
| Conditions | HOPs versus error bars in Experiment 1; HOPs at two frame rates versus a 50-line ensemble in Experiment 2. |
| Measurements | Binary trend judgment, accuracy as a function of log-likelihood evidence, threshold/spread of psychometric functions, and confidence. |
| Analysis | Participant-level psychometric modeling with order and learning checks. |

## Claim-level evidence

<a id="claim-kale-hop-threshold"></a>

### kale-hop-threshold

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** HOPs enabled correct trend discrimination at lower evidence levels than the static alternatives in the tested task.  
**Quantitative detail:** Effects are reported as psychometric-function threshold differences; consult the result figures for estimates and intervals.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/kale2019hypothetical.md#p001-b004) (passage); [PDF p. 6](../raw/kale2019hypothetical.md#p006-b002) (passage)
<a id="claim-kale-learning-qualification"></a>

### kale-learning-qualification

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** Practice and condition order affected performance, qualifying a simple encoding-only explanation.  
**Quantitative detail:** The paper reports no reliable PF-spread difference by visualization in Experiment 1 but an interaction with starting condition.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 6](../raw/kale2019hypothetical.md#p006-b002) (passage)


## Conclusions

- Sampling animation can support probabilistic trend inference by non-experts, but its advantage depends on task, exposure, timing, and comparison display. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 9](../raw/kale2019hypothetical.md#p009-b002) (passage)

## Limitations

- Frame rate, attention, learning/order, and confidence interpretation remain open; the task covers only binary noisy-trend inference. **Provenance:** `authors`. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 9](../raw/kale2019hypothetical.md#p009-b002) (passage)
