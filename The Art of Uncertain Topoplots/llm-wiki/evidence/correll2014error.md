## Research question

- How do conventional error bars and alternative distributional encodings affect probability judgments, inferential strategy, and confidence? **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/correll2014error.md#p001-b022) (passage)

## Contributions

- Tests behavioral consequences of bar-plus-error, modified-box, gradient, and violin encodings rather than assuming statistical equivalence implies perceptual equivalence. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/correll2014error.md#p001-b020) (passage); [PDF p. 1](../raw/correll2014error.md#p001-b022) (passage)

## Claim-level evidence

<a id="claim-correll-within-bar"></a>

### correll-within-bar

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** Bar-based containment induced a within-the-bar likelihood bias that symmetric alternative encodings mitigated.  
**Quantitative detail:** Direction and condition comparisons are reported with the experiment results.  
**Evidence:** [Fig. 5, PDF p. 6](../raw/correll2014error.md#p006-b013) (figure)
<a id="claim-correll-confidence"></a>

### correll-confidence

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** Confidence changed systematically with display-derived evidence, but the relationship depended on task strategy and encoding.  
**Quantitative detail:** One confidence analysis reports R²=.66 and β=-8.30; a separate CDF analysis reports R²=.805 and β=6.78.  
**Evidence:** [PDF p. 6](../raw/correll2014error.md#p006-b014) (passage); [Fig. 8, PDF p. 8](../raw/correll2014error.md#p008-b023) (figure)


## Conclusions

- Error-bar design is not neutral: alternative symmetric encodings can reduce containment bias, although the tested alternatives did not differ reliably on every outcome. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 9](../raw/correll2014error.md#p009-b002) (passage)

## Limitations

- Stylized online tasks and limited differences between alternatives constrain generalization to scientific-domain judgments. **Provenance:** `authors`. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 9](../raw/correll2014error.md#p009-b002) (passage)
