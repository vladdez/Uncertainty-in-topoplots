## Research question

- Can value-suppressing uncertainty palettes improve value–uncertainty integration and encourage decisions that account for uncertainty? **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/correll2018task.md#p001-b013) (passage); [PDF p. 6](../raw/correll2018task.md#p006-b006) (passage)

## Contributions

- Introduces VSUPs, which reduce value resolution as uncertainty increases, and tests identification and risk-framed prediction behavior against conventional bivariate or juxtaposed maps. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 1](../raw/correll2018task.md#p001-b013) (passage); [PDF p. 6](../raw/correll2018task.md#p006-b006) (passage); [PDF p. 7](../raw/correll2018task.md#p007-b008) (passage)

## Methods

| Component | Extracted detail |
| --- | --- |
| Design | Two within-participant crowdsourced experiments: identification and Battleship-like prediction. |
| Sample | 24 participants in each experiment; the two samples were separate. Identification sample: 12 women, 12 men, mean age 31 (SD 7.8). Prediction sample: 12 women, 12 men, mean age 37 (SD 9.8). |
| Conditions | Identification: eight combinations of juxtaposition/superposition, continuous/discrete encoding, quantization, and legend; eight replications (64 stimuli). Prediction: square or wedge legends crossed with VSUP or standard quantization. |
| Measurements | Identification accuracy and response time; prediction-token danger and uncertainty. Color-vision screening and exclusions were applied. |
| Analysis | Mixed/repeated-measures ANOVAs, post-hoc t tests, and a Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. |
| Evidence | [PDF p. 5](../raw/correll2018task.md#p005-b002) (passage); [PDF p. 6](../raw/correll2018task.md#p006-b006) (passage); [PDF p. 7](../raw/correll2018task.md#p007-b008) (passage); [PDF p. 8](../raw/correll2018task.md#p008-b002) (passage) |

## Claim-level evidence

<a id="claim-correll-superposition-accuracy"></a>

### correll-superposition-accuracy

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** For identification, superimposed displays were more accurate than juxtaposed displays in the reported comparison.  
**Quantitative detail:** 58% versus 51% accuracy; F(1,166)=5.5, p=.02.  
**Evidence:** [Figure 6, PDF p. 6](../raw/correll2018task.md#p006-b006) (figure); [PDF p. 8](../raw/correll2018task.md#p008-b002) (passage)
<a id="claim-correll-vsup-decisions"></a>

### correll-vsup-decisions

**Type:** `empirical_finding`  
**Claim:** VSUPs changed prediction choices toward lower uncertainty, but did not reduce average uncertainty in every aggregate test and produced a modest increase in average danger.  
**Quantitative detail:** Uncertainty-distribution difference D=.5, p=.03; mean danger .32 (SD .17) versus .29 (SD .18), t=2.3, p=.02; repeated-measures uncertainty effect F(1,61)=.05, p=.83.  
**Evidence:** [Figure 9, PDF p. 7](../raw/correll2018task.md#p007-b008) (figure); [PDF p. 8](../raw/correll2018task.md#p008-b002) (passage)

## Conclusions

- Task framing and integration demands affect whether VSUPs or other bivariate displays change judgments; the palette should not be treated as a universal solution. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 8](../raw/correll2018task.md#p008-b002) (passage); [PDF p. 9](../raw/correll2018task.md#p009-b002) (passage)

## Limitations

- The studies used small crowdsourced samples, specific palette constructions, and a loss-minimization game; results may change with correlated data, other risk frames, or other map types. **Provenance:** `authors`. **Evidence:** [PDF p. 7](../raw/correll2018task.md#p007-b008) (passage); [PDF p. 9](../raw/correll2018task.md#p009-b002) (passage)
